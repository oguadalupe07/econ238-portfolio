A NOTICE TO THE CAMPUS COMMUNITY

The Garbage Plate Mandate

One university. Three meals. One Rochester tradition.

What if the University of Rochester required every dining hall to serve only garbage plates for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, every single day?

(Yes, even breakfast.)

This would definitely make Rochester stand out, because what other university is forcing its students to eat the same local dish three times a day? It would bring students together through a shared tradition while resisting the same generic dining options you see everywhere.

But who actually pays for this tradition?

What about students with allergies, religious restrictions, or people who just don't like garbage plates? Even with different versions, you're taking away everyone's choice.

Students would probably spend more money eating off campus, while the university would face complaints, higher costs from dietary accommodations, and potentially fewer students wanting to attend (which honestly I wouldn't blame them).

Local restaurants and suppliers could benefit, but students and dining services would bear most of the costs. Overall, the economic and reputational consequences would probably outweigh the cultural benefits.

Without intervention, garbage plates would remain an occasional campus meal. A weekly garbage plate day would be cheaper and less controversial, although other universities could easily copy it.

Is preserving Rochester's identity really worth taking away students' freedom to choose what they eat?
